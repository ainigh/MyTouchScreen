# DECISIONS.md

One line per judgment call, newest at the bottom. All delegated by the owner.

## Concept
- Product name: **Wisp** — a small, calm light that follows your finger. The bundle/executable stays `GesturePointer` (as the build contract requires); "Wisp" is the display name, menu wording and overlay identity.
- Tone: quiet utility. No splash, no mascots; the overlay only says what changed, in two or three words.
- Accent color: Wisp Teal `#2EC4B6` (hover/engaged). State colors: pinch armed Amber `#FFB547`, dragging Violet `#8B7CF6`, scrolling Sky `#4D9DFF`, zooming Rose `#FF6B9A`, warning Coral `#FF6B5B`. All tested against both light and dark backgrounds by drawing on a translucent dark/light "puck" behind the ring.
- Typography: SF Pro Rounded (system font with `.rounded` design) for toasts and HUD labels; SF Mono (`monospacedSystemFont`) for numbers (fps, confidence, latency).
- Halo: 34 pt soft ring (2.5 pt stroke + 10 pt blurred glow) centered on the cursor; an inner progress arc fills as the pinch closes; a filled dot appears when the pinch is down.
- Menu-bar glyph: SF Symbols `hand.point.up.left` (standby), `hand.point.up.left.fill` (engaged), `hand.raised.slash` (off / missing permission).

## Gesture thresholds and feel
- Pointer filtering happens in *active-box units* (1.0 = full screen width), so settings are independent of display size. Defaults: 1€ minCutoff 0.6 Hz, beta 6, dCutoff 1 Hz — fast sweeps (screen width in 0.6 s) lag < 0.5 frame; chosen from a grid search in the test suite.
- Added a two-length "leash" dead zone after the 1€ filter: 0.0015 box while moving, 0.005 box (≈9 pt) once still for 250 ms. With Vision-like jitter of 6–8 pt RMS this holds the cursor at exactly 0 movement; the 1€ filter alone cannot get below ~1 pt/frame without becoming sluggish.
- Precision mode: below 0.12 box/s, hand motion is scaled by 0.45 (ramping smoothly to 1 at the threshold); the offset this creates is capped at 0.06 box and drained during fast movement so the absolute hand→screen mapping always comes back.
- Pinch: enter 0.35, exit 0.5 (normalized by wrist→middle-MCP), minimum 60 ms, refractory 120 ms counted from when the fingers opened (counting from the release *confirmation* swallowed real double pinches at 30 fps), release must persist 30 ms so a single glitched frame can't drop a drag.
- Pinch arm distance 0.65: as fingers close, the pointer anchor switches from the fingertip to the index knuckle plus the fingertip offset captured when the closure *began*; the click lands where the user was aiming even though the fingertip moves ~0.5 hand-sizes during a pinch. The pinch-start look-back walks back through recent samples while the fingers were further apart (≤ 400 ms).
- Drag: pinch held 250 ms, or moved > 0.012 box after the 100 ms freeze. Double click: within 400 ms and 2% of screen width; the second click reuses the first click's exact position.
- Right click fires on pinch confirmation (no hold semantics to wait for), so it feels instant.
- Fist = all four fingers' tip→wrist / MCP→wrist ratio < 1.2; extended > 1.45; poses need 80 ms to confirm. Pinches are ignored while the index is curled so a fist never clicks.
- Open palm engage: 600 ms to engage, 800 ms to disengage (slightly longer so an open hand used for pointing doesn't drop out), palm must be still (< 0.6 hand-sizes/s). The palm that toggled must change pose before it can toggle again.
- Hand dropout grace 150 ms (Vision misses frames); after that any held button/modifier is released. Lost for 2 s → disengage.
- Scroll: one box of fist travel = 1600 pt × speed; natural direction by default; momentum with exponential friction 4/s, starts above 120 pt/s, stops below 40 pt/s, integrated exactly so it is tick-rate independent.
- Zoom: ratio of the distance between the two pinch points, normalized by mean hand size (moving both hands toward the camera doesn't zoom). Zoom suppresses the primary hand's click.
- Display jump: 400 ms at an edge with a neighbour in that direction; the hand must return inside (8% margin) before jumping again, otherwise a hand resting at the edge would ping-pong.
- Dwell click (off by default): 800 ms within 0.8% of screen width; fires once, then needs movement.
- Real mouse wins: any real mouse movement suppresses hand output for 0.8 s and moves the pointer to the mouse's display.
- Default (uncalibrated) active box: 42% × 46% of the camera image centered at (0.5, 0.45), mirrored, with 4% overshoot past each edge so the menu bar and Dock are reachable without stretching.

## Technical
- Landmark coordinates are stored un-mirrored, top-left origin, normalized to the image; every distance is measured after undoing the 16:9 stretch (`HandFrame.imageAspect`), otherwise pinch and curl ratios change as the hand rotates.
- Calibration uses a homography (not bilinear) from the four pointed corners, because pointing from an angle produces a perspective-distorted quad. Mirroring falls out of the calibration for free.
- `GestureConfig` decoding is lenient (missing keys keep defaults) so exported settings survive app updates.
- Replay format: one JSON document, one frame per line, written incrementally; a file cut off by a crash is repaired on load by closing the array after the last complete frame.
- Threading: camera frame queue runs Vision synchronously (AVFoundation's `alwaysDiscardsLateVideoFrames` drops frames while it's busy, so nothing queues); frames hop to a serial pipeline queue that owns the state machine and posts CGEvents directly; the main thread only gets coalesced snapshots (at most one pending hop), so UI can never add cursor latency.
- Frame timestamps are `CACurrentMediaTime()` at arrival rather than sample-buffer PTS: the same clock as the momentum ticks, without depending on the capture session's clock.
- `CGEventSource.localEventsSuppressionInterval = 0`: by default synthetic events suppress the real mouse/keyboard for 250 ms, which would violate "never swallow real mouse input".
- Our events carry `eventSourceUserData = "WISP"`; the real-mouse monitor also ignores events within 2 pt of a position we posted in the last 0.5 s, in case the marker doesn't survive into NSEvent.
- Global hotkey uses Carbon `RegisterEventHotKey`: permission-free, consumes the keystroke, decades-stable. (Event tap / NSEvent monitor would leak the key to the front app.)
- Standby: when disengaged with palm-engage on, the camera keeps running but Vision only processes 8 fps (software throttle — changing the device frame rate at runtime risks AVFoundation exceptions). With palm-engage off, the camera stops completely. This resolves the spec's tension between "camera pauses when disengaged" and "engage by raising a palm". The camera light stays on in standby; README says so.
- Sounds are synthesized at launch from recipes in `GestureCore/SoundSynth.swift` (testable on Linux) instead of a build-time script: no binary assets, no extra build step that could fail.
- PiP preview mirrors the video with a layer transform and maps skeleton points itself (aspect-fill math), rather than relying on connection mirroring and `layerPointConverted`, whose behaviour with mirrored, y-up macOS layers I can't verify without a Mac.
- Mac UI classes are `@MainActor`; closures that run on background queues are built in `nonisolated static` helpers so they never inherit main-actor isolation; main hops use `MainActor.assumeIsolated` inside `DispatchQueue.main.async`.
- Display identity = display UUID (`CGDisplayCreateUUIDFromDisplayID`), so per-display calibration survives reboots and re-plugging.
- Replay in the app starts engaged and quits when done; `--dry-run` prints intents instead of posting. `gesture-replay` (Foundation-only executable) does the same on any OS.
- Added a GitHub Actions workflow (macOS 14 + Linux) so the Mac layer is actually compiled; the repo is private, so each push costs macOS runner minutes. The owner can delete `.github/workflows/build.yml` if unwanted.
- Swift tools version 5.9 (Swift 5 language mode) so the Mac layer's GCD/delegate code doesn't need Swift 6 strict-concurrency annotations to compile.

## Dropped or changed spec items
- App switcher (thumb + ring pinch → ⌘⇥, slide to browse) is implemented but **off by default**: Vision's ring-finger tip is the least reliable joint, and an accidental ⌘⇥ is disruptive. One toggle in Settings → Gestures.
- Zoom "per app" choice became one global setting (⌘+/⌘− keys by default, or ⌘+scroll): there's no reliable way to know which apps zoom on ⌘-scroll, and a hard-coded list would rot.
- "Camera pauses when disengaged" → standby at 8 fps Vision while palm-engage is on (see Technical); full stop when it's off or Wisp is turned off.
- Sounds generated at launch, not by a build script (see Technical).
- No app icon file: an `.icns` is a binary asset; the app lives in the menu bar and uses SF Symbols there.

## Install and updates (modelled on ainigh/tool-mac-tool)
- One-line install through `gh` (`install.sh`): the repository is private, so gh signed in is the one auth path that works for both the release download and the source tarball. Release first; build from source when there's no release, `WISP_FROM_SOURCE=1`, or `WISP_BRANCH` is set. Keeps the previous copy until the new one is seen running for 8 s, else restores it.
- Installs into `~/Applications` (no admin rights, same as tool-mac-tool); `make install` now uses the same place so there's one copy to grant permissions to.
- In-app updater follows the branch recorded in Info.plist (`WispBranch`) and compares the branch head with `WispCommit`; prebuilt when the latest release was made from that exact commit, else builds here with `scripts/make-app.sh`. The decision logic (`UpdatePlan`, `Version`, `Release`, `Commit`, Swift version parsing) lives in GestureCore and is tested on Linux.
- Release versions are `1.0.<CI run number>`; local/source builds are `1.0-<short sha>`.
- `make-app.sh` now writes to `build/` and produces `GesturePointer.zip`, so CI, the installer and the updater all build the same way. Requires Swift ≥ 5.9 (the package's tools version), with an installed-Xcode fallback.
- Optional `WISP_SIGN_IDENTITY` / `signIdentity` default: ad-hoc signatures change every build, so macOS forgets Camera/Accessibility grants after an update; a stable self-made code-signing certificate keeps them. Optional because creating a certificate needs the user.
- The updater disengages Wisp before restarting so no button or modifier can be left held.
- CI back to running on every push (as in tool-mac-tool) since releases come from pushes to main; when Actions isn't available, installs and updates fall back to building on the Mac.
- When a different hand takes over as the pointing hand, any drag/scroll/zoom/app-switch the old hand held is cancelled immediately (mouse-up / scroll ended), since the reset detector will never report its end.
- A drag that began while real-mouse priority suppressed output sends neither down nor up; the mouse-up is only emitted if its mouse-down was sent.
- Out-of-range imported settings are bounded where they could crash or NaN: app-switcher step floor 0.01 box, momentum friction floor 0.1/s, non-positive box scale treated as 1; replay frames with non-positive image aspect are rejected.
- Updater verifies the new bundle before swapping it in: same bundle id, `WispCommit` equal to the commit being installed, and `codesign --verify --deep --strict` (catches truncated or tampered bundles; authenticity rests on HTTPS/gh from GitHub). It looks for `GesturePointer.app` (what make-app.sh zips), not the installed copy's possibly renamed bundle name.
- The camera preview pins its connection's mirroring off; the mirror comes only from the layer transform, so the skeleton overlay always lines up whatever the per-camera default is.
- check.sh uses POSIX `[[:space:]]` rather than `\s` so `make check` also works with macOS's BSD sed.
- The camera's data-output connection pins mirroring off too (automatic adjustment disabled), so Vision always sees the unmirrored image GestureCore assumes, whatever the camera.
- Camera asks for 60 fps only when a frame-rate range of the chosen format contains it; otherwise it takes the format's own maximum, since an unsupported frame duration raises an uncatchable exception.
- InputSynth permits local mouse/keyboard events in the remote-mouse-drag suppression state as well, so the real mouse keeps working during a pinch-drag.
- Quit (menu, Ctrl-C or SIGTERM) disengages the state machine, releases held buttons/modifiers and stops InputSynth posting before exit; the hotkey always disengages, even if camera access was revoked.
- If the real mouse moves during a hand drag, the synthetic button is released at the real mouse position and the rest of that pinch is ignored, so the real mouse never drags what the hand grabbed.
- Launch at login is read back from `SMAppService` after every change (an observable model, re-read when the app activates), never assumed from the request, so a failed change can't leave the toggle showing the wrong state; "requires approval" opens Login Items and says so.
- After a capture-session runtime error the camera reopens its device and restarts, backing off 0.5–8 s, at most 5 times within 30 s before giving up with a message; turning Wisp off and on, or picking a camera, retries. The notification is observed by its string name because its Swift spelling differs between SDKs.
- A camera that connects is used again when it's the saved choice (Continuity iPhone back in range) or when no camera is working; a failed configure drops the stale input so `start()` retries.
- The 120 Hz tick timer runs only while a scroll glide is in progress (`needsTicks`), not for the whole time the hand is engaged.
- Changing settings mid-glide retunes the momentum instead of replacing it, so the glide still sends its momentum-ended phase.

## M7 — Velocity gestures (SPEC-M7)
- Velocity mode is the default `inputModel` (tests pass); Pose mode is one segmented picker away. Settings files from before M7 decode as Velocity with default `VelocityParams` (lenient decoding now merges nested objects key by key).
- Velocity mode still runs the pose detector every frame, but only for the open-palm engage/disengage; its pinch/fist events are ignored.
- Hand space = camera image mirrored (+x = the user's right) and isotropic (y divided by the 16:9 aspect), so 0.1 means the same distance in both axes.
- Velocity: least-squares slope over the samples of the last 40 ms (3 frames at 60 fps, 2 at 30 fps; at least 2 after a dropped frame). Shorter windows were too noisy at 30 fps; longer ones smeared a tap's turnaround.
- Frame counts are time at a 60 Hz reference: `framesEnter` 2 = 33 ms = two frames at 60 fps, one frame at 30 fps. Coherence: each frame of a run is within `coherenceAngle` of the previous frame's direction; a first fast frame after a still hand (no direction yet) counts as coherent. Requiring two frames above `vEnter` at 30 fps would need taps longer than `clickMaxPath` allows (a 0.6-hand-size tap is only ~2 frames fast at 30 fps).
- `framesExit` default 4 instead of 2: a tap reverses at its lowest point and the speed dips below `vExit` for up to ~2 frames there; with 2 the out and back strokes became two segments.
- Segment duration `T` runs from the first fast frame to the first frame of the final slow run (the exit hold doesn't count against `clickMaxDuration`).
- Anchor = the last still sample (< `preClickStillV`) if it was within 100 ms of the first fast frame, else the frame before it. By the first frame above `vEnter` the hand has already moved ~0.3 hand sizes (≈100 pt), which put clicks visibly off-target and differed between 30 and 60 fps.
- Dominant axis comes from the furthest point of the segment (excursion), not the end point: a tap returns, so its net displacement has no direction. Scroll uses the excursion when the hand came back and the net displacement otherwise. "Reversed" = the end is within `returnFraction` of the excursion (came back at least half way).
- Clicks are posted immediately (count 1), and a second tap within `doubleClickWindow` of the first click (measured to the second tap's first fast frame — measured to its classification the window could never be met) and within `doubleClickDisp` posts count 2 at the first click's exact point. No click waits for a possible drag or double click.
- Drag = click, then (after the tap settles below `dragMoveMin`) movement above `dragMoveMin` for `dragConfirmTime` (new parameter, 0.10 s) starting within `dragStartWindow`. The confirm time keeps the rising edge of a double click's second tap (which passes `dragMoveMin` on its way to `vEnter`) from starting a drag. A post-click segment that classifies as reposition/scroll inside the window also starts the drag (press at the click, catch up to the hand). While dragging, only a tap-shaped segment is "another flick" and drops (at that tap's anchor); scroll- or reposition-shaped segments are drag motion. Mouse-down is posted with click state 1, so the preceding click doesn't turn the drag into a double-click-drag.
- Two-hand zoom: both hands' segments start within `twoHandWindow`; the zoom begins once the centroid spread has changed by 8% and sends the spread ratio per frame; both segments are consumed (no click/scroll). The cursor is held during a zoom and re-anchored after.
- Cursor warp: after a click/right click/scroll/drop, one `move` to the anchor, then the fingertip mapping keeps a box-space offset so the cursor doesn't jump back to the hand. The offset drains by the distance the hand travels outside flicks (fully after half a box width), so absolute reach returns. Display jumps clear it.
- Scroll output: `began`, one `changed` frame of the flick velocity, `ended`, then momentum (`ScrollMomentum`, exact exponential integral, so tick-rate independent) at `momentumDecay` 0.92 per 1/60 s → friction 5.0/s. Ticks keep using the pipeline's 120 Hz timer (≥ display rate on every Mac it runs on), only while gliding. Content direction = flick direction with natural scrolling; "Follow the system scroll direction" (default on) reads `com.apple.swipescrolldirection` and is re-checked every 2 s.
- Noise rejection: low-confidence frames are skipped by the velocity logic; a segment is ignored if a skipped frame touches its anchor (from just before the anchor to the first fast frame) or immediately precedes its peak frame. A confident jump > `glitchDisp` resets velocity, run and segment. Refractory starts at the emitted click/scroll; fast frames during it can't build a run.
- Hand assignment: with one hand in view, the pointing hand may now jump up to 0.6 image widths between frames (was 0.25): a 30 fps flick moves the palm a third of the image per frame and was being treated as a new hand, which reset the gesture.
- Synthetic generator lives in `VelocitySynthetic.swift` (not `Replay.swift`): minimum-jerk strokes, hand sizes 0.10–0.14, per-joint noise 0.0006–0.0014 (jitter: 0.002–0.0035 plus 4% low-confidence scattered frames and 1% confident 3-hand-size glitches), slow sway, ±2 ms timestamp jitter, 1% dropped frames, 30 and 60 fps. Taps: 0.55–0.75 hand sizes out in 60–85 ms and back in 70–100 ms — what the spec's `vEnter`/`clickMaxPath` imply. Fast vertical repositions stay below `scrollMinPeak` (a fast vertical flick *is* a scroll by the spec's table); drags set off within 0.2 s of the tap.
- Acceptance numbers on the synthetic set (200 samples per class, half 30 fps): click 197, right-click 199, double-click 193, scroll-up/down 200, reposition-fast/slow 200, fine-target 200, drag 199, rest 200, jitter 200, out-of-frame 200, zoom 200. Defaults unchanged from the spec except `framesExit` (4) and the added `dragConfirmTime`.
- "`rest` and `jitter` produce zero intents" is tested as zero non-move intents: the synthetic rest includes real slow sway, which the cursor is supposed to follow.
- Guided recording: the timetable is `TuningScript` (GestureCore, tested); the app engages itself, posts no input during the session (so flicks can't click anything), keeps the camera at full rate even if the palm disengages, and writes `segments` after the frames when it finishes (or the prompts finished so far if stopped or quit early). `zoom` is a synthetic-only label (needs two hands; not prompted).
- `tune` grid: `vEnter` {0.7, 0.9, 1.1} with `vExit` = ½ `vEnter`, `clickMaxDuration` {0.14, 0.18, 0.22}, `clickMaxDisp` {0.7, 0.9, 1.1}, `scrollMinDisp` {1.2, 1.6, 2.0}, `scrollMinPeak` {1.4, 1.8, 2.2} — 243 runs, ~6 s on a 5-minute session in release. F1 counts a do-nothing prompt that triggered anything as a false positive; ties keep the current values.
- No owner recordings yet, so `recordings/` holds only a README; the test that replays committed recordings passes vacuously until one is added.
- "Precision slow-gain" is the existing Pointer → Precision setting (it applies to both modes), renamed in the UI rather than duplicated.
- Velocity mode has no app switcher (it was a ring-finger pinch); the toggle is hidden in Velocity mode.
- Guided session clock starts on the first frame with a hand, not at launch: camera start-up (longer with Continuity Camera) would otherwise eat the first prompt. During the session the click sound marks each recording window only; at the end Wisp disengages before input posting is switched back on, so the real cursor isn't handed to a hand that was performing prompts. The prompt timer runs in common run-loop modes so it keeps updating while the menu is open.

## Hold & wiggle (owner request after M7)
- New default input model `dwellShake` ("Hold & wiggle"): any movement moves the cursor; holding still 2 s clicks; 3 direction changes along one axis within 1 s scrolls; palm engage unchanged. Added as a third model rather than replacing Velocity, so Velocity and Pose stay selectable.
- "Changes direction" is measured on the palm centroid (steadier than the fingertip), per axis, with a minimum stroke of 0.25 hand sizes before a turn counts, so tracker jitter and small corrections never add up to a wiggle. Three changes = four strokes (down-up-down-up).
- What a wiggle does: it enters scroll mode (the wiggle itself has no net direction to scroll by). The cursor goes back to where the wiggle started and holds; the palm's movement along the wiggle's axis then scrolls like a fist scroll (content follows the hand with natural scrolling, sideways wiggle → horizontal scroll only); holding still for 0.5 s (palm < 0.5 hand sizes/s) ends it. No momentum: scrolling ends when the hand is still.
- Click: the existing dwell click with 2 s and a 0.8%-of-screen-width stillness radius (the rest-lock dead zone keeps a still hand's cursor exactly still). It fires once, then the cursor must leave the radius and settle again. After engaging (or after the real mouse took over) the cursor must move first, so engaging with a still hand never clicks.
- A quick double tap (down-up-down-up) is itself three direction changes and scrolls in this model — inherent to the rule.
- Right click, drag and zoom aren't in this model; the Settings help text says to switch models for them. The Velocity guided recording and `tune` keep tuning Velocity mode (they force it).
- All five values (hold time, radius, changes, window, smallest stroke, plus the scroll stop time) are in Settings → Gestures → Hold & wiggle with a reset button.
- Hold & wiggle, completed: the right hand points, the left hand is a modifier whose mere presence (any pose, anywhere) matters. Left up → right-hand movement drags; left down → drop; left up + wiggle → zoom (then up/right = in, down/left = out; 0.25 screen widths of palm travel doubles). No right click. Velocity and Pose remain selectable but are no longer what anyone gets by default.
- Which hand is which: with two hands, position (the user's right hand is on the camera's left) — more reliable than Vision's chirality; with one hand, its chirality (a lone left hand never points). "Pointing hand: Left" swaps the roles. Only the pointing hand's open palm engages/disengages.
- The left hand counts as up after 0.1 s in view and as gone after 0.25 s missing, so a one-frame ghost can't start a drag and a one-frame dropout can't drop one.
- With the left hand up the button doesn't go down immediately: it waits until the right hand has moved 0.6 hand sizes, or moved at least 0.15 and then held still 0.3 s (or the left hand drops after such a move). Then it presses where the cursor was when the left hand came up and catches up to the cursor. That's what lets "left up + wiggle = zoom" start without pressing anything; once dragging, wiggles are drag motion.
- After a scroll or zoom ends with the left hand still up, a fresh drag is armed from the current cursor.
- Multiple displays: the hand drives the largest display (by area) and stays there — no edge jumps, and the real mouse no longer moves it to another display. `stayOnLargestDisplay` (Settings → Pointer) turns the old edge-jump behaviour back on.
- Parked for later (owner): calibration as "show me what scroll, click, drag, drop and zoom look like for you" instead of pointing at corners.
- Owner's first recording (`recordings/owner-freeform-2026-10-12.json`, free-form, 83 s at 30 fps): in the right-hand-only stretches the palm barely moved (±0.005) while the index fingertip swung 0.05–0.1 image widths side to side or up and down, so palm-based wiggle detection found nothing. Hold & wiggle now follows the fingertip for everything — wiggle detection, scroll/zoom movement, the "hold still" exits, and the drag-commit distance.
- From the same recording: a resting real fingertip jitters at 0.35–0.8 hand sizes/s, so "holding still" (ends scroll/zoom, commits a small drag) is now < 1.2 hand sizes/s (was 0.5, which never ended a scroll). A calm hand drifts the cursor 25–40 pt over ~1.5 s, so the dwell-click radius is now 2.5% of the screen width (was 0.8%, ≈14 pt, which a real hand almost never stays inside for 2 s). The session never held still for 2 s, so it says nothing yet about the click itself.
- The fingertip also jumps ~0.6 hand sizes within two frames when the index finger bends (seen repeatedly at 53–58 s). Left as is: it's real pointer movement and the user's intent there is unknown without labels.
- Multiple displays (owner, replaces "largest display"): the hand stays on the display the real mouse pointer is on when Wisp engages — by palm (the app keeps the machine told where the mouse is while disengaged) or by hotkey/menu. No edge jumps, and touching the real mouse on another display while engaged doesn't move the hand's display. `stayOnEngagedDisplay` off restores edge jumps and following the mouse.

## Hold & wiggle: lock, wiggle click, fist drag, two-fist zoom/scroll, two-palm toggle (owner request)
- Holding the cursor still 1 s locks it (same stillness radius as before). When the hand next moves (0.12 hand sizes from where it rested) the cursor stays locked 1 s more: the wake window. A wiggle in it clicks at the locked point; a fist in it presses there. Nothing in it and the hand went somewhere (≥ 0.25 hand sizes): the cursor unlocks and goes straight to where the hand points (a pointer, so the move made during the window isn't lost); a fidget or an unfinished wiggle keeps it locked.
- The 2 s dwell click and the wiggle-to-scroll mode are gone from this model: the wiggle-in-the-wake-window is the only click. A wiggle while the cursor is moving does nothing.
- Click wiggle = 2 changes of direction (left-right-left) within 1 s, strokes ≥ 0.25 hand sizes. Two needs only one back-and-forth more than a correction, but ordinary pointing never produces it from a rested hand (synthetic reposition/fine-target/tap samples: zero clicks). The setting goes 1–5.
- After a click the cursor stays locked and waits for the hand to stop (fingertip < 1.2 hand sizes/s) before the next movement counts, so the tail of the wiggle never unlocks it. A second wiggle within 1.5 s at the same spot is a double click (click count 2): the user asked for the wiggle to be the only way to click, so double click needed a form.
- Fist drag: the fist must form while locked or in the wake window, with the other hand out of view (so starting a two-fist gesture never presses). While dragging the cursor follows the palm (the index tip curls away), offset so it starts exactly at the pressed point; the same lock rules apply, so a resting fist locks and waits a second after moving on. Opening the fist drops at the cursor — the locked point if it rested — and leaves the cursor locked (opening the hand swings the fingertip out). A fist while the cursor moves does nothing.
- The other hand no longer modifies anything (left-hand drag and wiggle-zoom removed); it only takes part in the two-palm toggle and two-fist gestures. `dragReady` state replaced by `twoFists`.
- Two fists (`TwoFistGesture`): nothing happens while they move; when both rest (speed < 1 hand size/s for 0.3 s) the last stroke decides — the spread changed most → zoom by (spread after / spread at the stroke's start)^gain; the midpoint moved most → scroll along its main axis, box delta × the usual scroll scale and direction. "Last stroke" = since the last turn-back of ≥ 0.3 hand sizes, so close-widen-rest zooms in again and raise-lower-rest scrolls down. Speed is measured over 0.15 s, not frame to frame: two hands' landmark jitter made a per-frame speed never look still.
- Two-fist scroll is delivered as a 15% step plus a momentum glide for the rest, so a whole stroke's scroll doesn't land as one jump; zoom is one began/changed/ended burst (keyboard zoom turns it into ⌘= / ⌘− steps).
- Engage/disengage needs both hands as open palms held still (all input models); one palm does nothing. A missing or unclassified hand for up to 0.3 s neither restarts the hold nor re-arms the toggle (Vision drops hands; a re-seen hand takes a few frames to classify). While both palms are up the cursor is held locked.
- The owner recording engaged with one palm, so its regression test engages via the hotkey; under the new rules it produces one press/release (a real fist after a rest at ~16 s) and nothing else.
- Velocity and Pose models keep their own click/drag/scroll gestures (they are opt-in alternatives); only engagement changed there.

## One gesture model: every other way of clicking removed (owner request)
- The owner found the other click gestures got in the way: hands moved like that without meaning to click. So the lock-then-wiggle click is now the only click, and everything else that could press a button is gone: Pose mode (pinch click, double pinch, middle-pinch right click, pinch-hold drag, fist scroll, two-hand pinch zoom, ring-pinch app switcher), Velocity mode (tap click / double / right click, tap-drag, flick scroll, two-hand spread zoom), and the optional dwell click. With their clicks gone neither model had a purpose, so the input-model switch is gone too; Hold & wiggle is simply how Wisp works.
- Removed with them: `VelocityEngine`/`Classifier`/`Tracker`/`Params`, `TuningScript`, the `tune` executable and `tools/tune.md`, the guided recording (`--script`), the overlay's flick trail, result flashes and "why not?" readout, the pinch progress arc, the pinch calibration step (calibration is corners only; older saved profiles still decode, their pinch fields ignored), the `rightClick` and `key` intents (and InputSynth's right-button and ⌘-hold code), and the pinch/app-switcher/dwell/velocity settings. Old settings files still import: unknown keys are ignored.
- `GestureDetector` is now just the pose classifier (point / fist / open palm with a confirmation delay). An open palm still requires the thumb clear of every fingertip (`palmThumbClearance`, the old pinch-exit distance).
- The wiggle click now flashes a ripple at the click point (double ripple for a double click), kept from the Velocity overlay, so the only click is visible as well as audible.
- A fist only presses if it was made after the cursor locked: a fist still held after the real mouse interrupted a drag never presses again by itself (found while porting the safety tests).
- `MotionSynthetic` (was `VelocitySynthetic`) keeps the realistic synthetic motions; the tests use them, plus pinch taps, a held pinch and a 5 s still hand, to check that none of the retired click gestures clicks any more.

## Accessibility survives updates (owner report: had to remove and re-add after installing)
- Cause: an ad-hoc signature's designated requirement is the binary's cdhash. macOS stores that requirement with the Accessibility grant, so every new build failed it — the switch stayed on in System Settings but `AXIsProcessTrusted()` was false until the entry was removed and re-added.
- Fix: `make-app.sh` (used by CI, the installer and the in-app updater) now signs ad-hoc with an explicit designated requirement `identifier "app.wisp.GesturePointer"`, which every future build satisfies. Trade-off: any app with that identifier would match the grant — acceptable for a personal tool, and a real certificate (`WISP_SIGN_IDENTITY` / the `signIdentity` default) still takes precedence.
- Grants made to older builds were stored with the old cdhash requirement, so the first update after this change can still lapse once. For that (and any future lapse) the app remembers that Accessibility worked before; launching untrusted after that runs `tccutil reset Accessibility app.wisp.GesturePointer` (only our own entry, no admin rights) and re-prompts, once per lapse. The Setup window's Accessibility button also clears the stale entry before prompting.

## Wiggle any time, fast motion freezes the cursor (owner request: "try another way for click")
- No lock and no wake window: a wiggle clicks whenever it happens. The owner's idea for keeping the click on target: whenever the hand moves fast — as fast as a wiggle — the cursor doesn't move. So the cursor freezes while the tracked point is faster than `freezeSpeed` (2.5 hand sizes/s; a click wiggle's strokes run at about 3–6, calm pointing in the owner's recording mostly 1–2.5) and follows again after 0.15 s below 1.2 hand sizes/s.
- It freezes where it was *before* the burst: the last position while the hand was below half the freeze speed, taken from the frame before (the speed estimate trails the hand by a frame), within 0.3 s. A frame that alone looks fast holds the cursor for that frame too, so a burst never flicks the cursor before the freeze confirms.
- After a freeze the cursor carries on from where it stopped (like lifting a mouse), rather than jumping to where the hand now points: a fast move leaves the cursor where it was, which is what "the mouse should not move" asks for. The offset this leaves drains away with real hand movement (only above 1.2 hand sizes/s — resting jitter used to drain it and slid the cursor ~150 pt), so the reach to the screen edges comes back.
- The click lands at the frozen point, or — for a wiggle slow enough not to freeze — where the cursor was when the wiggle's first stroke began.
- Only the index finger and the fist are tracked: two-fist zoom/scroll (`TwoFistGesture`) is removed. The other hand still takes part in the two-open-palms toggle, kept because the hand needs a way to disengage (CLAUDE.md) and it can't be confused with pointing. The scroll/zoom output path (intents, InputSynth, `ScrollMomentum`, the tick timer) stays in place for a future scroll gesture; nothing produces scroll or zoom now, and their settings are gone from the UI.
- A fist presses any time (no lock needed) where the cursor was before the hand closed (the frozen point, or 0.2 s before the fist was confirmed), but only after it is held 0.25 s (`fistHoldTime`): the owner's recording has fists flickering for 0.1–0.2 s while the hand moves. The cursor holds still while the fist is pending. A fist already made when engaging, after the hand was lost, or after the real mouse interrupted a drag never presses until the hand is seen open (raw pose, not the confirmed one, so the confirmation delay can't clear it).
- Without the lock, tracker glitches matter: frames under 0.5 confidence are skipped for motion; speed is the slower of the last two two-frame speeds (a one-frame jump makes only one fast); the wiggle detector sees a median-of-3 fingertip, ignores strokes under 0.07 s, and a turn the hand rested at for more than 0.2 s (`maxPause`) starts a new wiggle instead of counting — two pinches or taps a second apart aren't a wiggle. 200 synthetic samples per motion class (reposition, fine target, taps, flicks, rest, jitter with garbage/glitch frames, out of frame): 0 clicks, 0 presses; quick double taps 1 click in 200.
- The owner recording, replayed: 17 clicks from the finger wags (expected — a wiggle now clicks any time) and presses only during held fists (≈13 s, 16 s, 57 s, 60–62 s).

## Laser pointer (owner request: "it should feel like I'm holding a laser light to the screen", "an invisible stick", "when I'm stable it still moves or doesn't move enough", "sometimes it just stops and I manually engage")
- The cursor is absolute: wherever the fingertip points through the calibration, at every speed. Removed: precision mode (slow moves at 0.45× with an offset up to 6% of the screen — "doesn't move enough", "rubber"), the fast-motion freeze (it held the cursor whenever the hand moved faster than ~2.5 hand sizes/s, i.e. crossing the screen in under ~2 s, and only let go after 0.15 s below 1.2 — "it just stops"), and the carry-on offset after a freeze. Their settings are gone; old settings files still import (unknown keys ignored).
- What still holds the cursor, briefly and on purpose: a wiggle click (on the click point), a forming fist and a drop. The hold ends when the hand has been below 1.2 hand sizes/s for 0.15 s, or after 0.6 s at most, and the cursor then glides back under the fingertip over 0.15 s. A one-frame jump (≥ 5 hand sizes/s while the frames around it are slow) is held for that frame as a tracker glitch.
- Wiggle click point: the cursor follows the wag now, so the click goes where the cursor was at the last calm moment before the wag (per-frame cursor speed ≤ 10% of the stroke's top speed, before its final fast stretch into the turn; the turn itself is slow and skipped). Needed because the move that brings the hand to a target and a first wag in the same direction are one stroke to the detector. Clicks land within ~6 pt of the arrival point in the tests. A second wiggle within 1.5 s and 5% of the screen width (was 2.5%) is a double click and lands exactly on the first click.
- Rest lock rebuilt: before, the lock only engaged once the cursor stayed within the *moving* leash (≈2 pt) for 0.25 s, which a real fingertip (≈5 pt of jitter) never does — so the still cursor wandered. Now: the filtered point staying within `restDeadZoneBox` of its 0.5 s average for 0.2 s locks the cursor onto that average; a locked cursor steps onto the average only when it is half the radius away (a slow creep); leaving the lock needs a move of twice the radius or one that lasts 60 ms. On a still hand with 6 pt RMS noise the cursor makes one 4 pt settling step in 29 s (with the old lock trigger the same filter wandered ~100 pt in 5 s); a 17 pt nudge moves it 17 pt.
- 1€ retuned on the owner recording (moving error vs a centred 5-frame average) and synthetic still-hand noise: min cutoff 0.6 Hz, beta 14 (was 6; with the lock doing the resting, more beta costs nothing at rest), moving leash 0.001 box, rest radius 0.005 box (≈9 pt).
- Settings: one *Steadiness* slider (Snappy ↔ Steady) sets min cutoff (1.2 → 0.3 Hz, geometric), beta (20 → 8) and the rest radius (0.003 → 0.007); its middle is the default. CLAUDE.md asks for min cutoff and beta in settings; the owner found the separate sliders confusing, so they are behind this one knob (and in the exported JSON).
- Settings window: Pointer (how it works, Steadiness, hand movement to cover the screen, pointing hand, the display option only with 2+ displays), Camera (unchanged, calibration), General (shortcut + palms toggle, feedback, system, settings file). The Gestures tab (freeze speeds, wiggle counts/windows/stroke, double-click window, fist hold) is gone from the UI; its values stay in `config.shake` and the JSON export.
- Saved smoothing from earlier versions is reset to the new defaults once (`pointerTuningVersion` = 2): the owner's recording shows beta at 1.39 (default was 6), i.e. a very laggy cursor left over from the old sliders. Reach, calibration and everything else are kept.
- Hand loss never disengages (the 2 s `handLostTimeout` is gone, as is `DisengageReason.handLost`): only the palms, the shortcut or the menu turn Wisp off. A real loss still drops a drag after 0.15 s. After 2 s without a hand the snapshot says `handAway` and the app tracks at 15 fps instead of full rate (CPU), back to full on the first frame with a hand.
- A lone hand always points, whatever chirality Vision gives it: a flip to "left" used to make the cursor ignore the hand. With two hands, position still decides.
- Cursor-speed presets (`PointerResponse`): nine slow-gain/fast-gain pairs blended by hand speed (0.15 → 1.0 screen widths/s), chosen in Settings → Pointer or the menu's Cursor Speed submenu. Default stays Direct (laser pointer). Non-direct presets move the cursor by scaled motion and clamp at the screen edge, like a mouse; after a click hold they continue from the held point instead of gliding back under the finger.
- Gesture presets (`ClickGesture`, `DragGesture`, `ZoomGesture`, all in `GestureConfig`; Settings → Gestures uses radio groups, and the menu bar has submenus): defaults stay wiggle / hold fist / zoom off, so nothing changes until the owner picks one. Pinches, the thumb trigger and the fist are all `ContactSensor`s: debounced, with hysteresis, and armed only after being seen open. A click-only contact clicks once it's confirmed (0.05 s). A drag-only contact presses after being held. A contact that does both clicks on a quick release and drags when held. Clicks land where the cursor was just before the hand changed shape. Zoom holds the cursor and blocks the pointing hand's clicks and presses while a two-hand zoom forms. The thumb-trigger thresholds (0.5 / 0.65 hand sizes, thumb tip to index knuckle) are a first guess; record a session if they misfire.

## Target assist (owner request: "if the mouse is heading towards something or passes around a control, then maybe it can slow down and even go closer to the closest element … prioritize elements more likely to be clicked … settings to make it dynamic so I can experiment")
- `TargetAssist` (GestureCore) runs after the smoother, in screen points, on the clickable things the app finds around the pointer. Three stages: **slowdown** (lower control-display gain near a target, to 0.2× at full strength), **catch-up** (once clear of targets, part of the hand's own travel brings the cursor back under the finger — never on its own while the hand is still, so the cursor stays rock steady), **magnet** (a slowing cursor within `magnet × reach` of a target is pulled into its middle 40%; the pull is recomputed every frame, not accumulated, so moving away always lets go).
- Heading: targets ahead along the direction of travel act from further away (look-ahead 0.2 s of travel, up to 3× the reach); ones beside the path are discounted by `anticipation`; flicks over 1500 pt/s fade out all slowdown (gone by 3500) so crossing the screen is never hindered.
- Never a trap: a target lets go once the finger itself is 30–120 pt past its reach, and the slowdown fades out as the cursor falls 45–110 pt behind the finger (hold-back budget). The cursor never strays more than 160 pt from the finger. First version without these left a full-strength cursor sitting in a button while the hand moved 140 pt on.
- "More likely to be clicked": kind (button 1.0, menu 0.95, toggle 0.9, link 0.85, field 0.7, other 0.6, row 0.5) × size (full help up to 24 pt, down to 0.6 at 120 pt; anything over 600 pt or 300×300 isn't a control) × default button (1.15) × clicked in the last 10 min (1.2). One target acts at a time; another takes over only when it ranks 30% better, so neighbours don't tug the cursor back and forth.
- Off while dragging (a drag follows the fist as is), while the cursor is held after a click, and reset on engage, display jumps and real-mouse takeover.
- Defaults are on, moderate (slowdown 0.5, magnet 0.4, reach 36 pt, anticipation 0.5, catch-up 0.5) for buttons, links, menus, toggles/tabs and fields; list rows and generic pressables are off by default (dense lists would make the cursor sticky everywhere). Every knob is in Settings → Targets and the exported JSON; the menu has an on/off item.
- Mac side (`TargetScanner`): Accessibility only (no screenshots, no ML). Every 150 ms while engaged, on its own queue with a 50 ms AX messaging timeout and a 25 ms / 2500-element budget, it walks the window (or menu bar, or Dock) under the pointer — found by hit-testing — skipping subtrees whose frame is more than 420 pt away, plus the clickable ancestor of the element under the pointer. Skips rescans while the pointer has moved < 24 pt in the same window and the last scan is < 0.6 s old. Tables and lists use their visible rows. Stops (and clears the targets) when disengaged, off, or without Accessibility.
- Chromium/Electron apps get `AXManualAccessibility = true` once (their documented switch to build the tree). `AXEnhancedUserInterface` is deliberately not set: it is known to break window animations and window managers. Trade-off: such an app keeps its accessibility tree built for the rest of its run, which costs it some CPU (as any screen reader or window manager would).
- Elements scrolled out of a scroll area or web page still report on-screen frames (under toolbars and other panes), so the walk clips each subtree to the visible part of its enclosing `AXScrollArea` / `AXWebArea` and window (an open `AXMenu` starts unclipped: it hangs outside the menu bar item that owns it). The scan's time budget is checked on every element (each is an IPC round trip, up to the 50 ms timeout against a hung app) and starts once the window is found. A recording started mid-session begins with the targets already in play.
- Low-confidence caveat, unverified on a Mac: if hit-testing at the pointer ever lands on Wisp's own click-through overlay, the scan falls back to the focused app's focused window (menu bar, Dock and background windows then get no help). "Outline every target found" shows it at a glance.
- Targets ride along in the next `TrackingFrame` (`"g"` in recordings, only when they changed), so `--record` captures them and `--replay` / `gesture-replay` reproduce target assist exactly on Linux.
- Overlay: a soft outline on the target being drawn to (Settings → Targets, on by default) and an optional faint outline of every target found, for tuning.

## Straight-line trajectory, on by default (owner request: "let mouse only move vertically or horizontally … picks based on the dominant direction … changes only when there is a sharp turn … a snake motion horizontally is horizontally straight … let this setting be cross cutting, about trajectory")
- `TrajectoryLock` (GestureCore) runs right after the smoother and speed gain and before target assist, in screen points, so it shapes every cursor path: any `PointerResponse`, target assist and fist drags. `GestureConfig.trajectory` = `.straight` (default) or `.free`; saved settings from before get `.straight` too (lenient decoding), as asked.
- Axis choice: a movement from rest (no 4 pt of travel within 0.15 s for 0.25 s) takes the axis the hand mostly moves along. While moving, the axis switches only when, over the last 0.15 s, the hand went ≥ 12 pt across it at an angle steeper than the turn angle — 35° … 75° from the `turnSharpness` slider, 55° by default. A 45° diagonal therefore never flips, and a snake or arc stays on its axis.
- Only the active axis moves the cursor. The dropped motion across it leaves the cursor off the finger; moving along an axis makes that axis's difference up at 0.8 of the hand's travel (never on its own, so a still hand keeps a still cursor). On the owner's free-form recording (lots of diagonal moves) the cursor sits a median 61 pt from where the finger points (81 pt at 0.5, 57 pt at 1.0 — diminishing returns, and faster catch-up visibly races). Screen edges clamp, like a mouse.
- After a click / press / drop hold, straight mode carries on from the held point (as the scaled speeds do) instead of gliding back under the finger: a glide is a diagonal move, bent onto one axis it moved a still cursor (caught by the fist-drop test).
- The laser-pointer tests in `WiggleClickTests` (cursor lands exactly where the finger points) run with `.free`; `TrajectoryLockTests` covers straight lines (snake, curve, diagonal, sharp turn, sharpness setting, fresh pick after rest, jitter, defaults, the machine path and click-then-still). The owner recording replays with the same 17 clicks and 5 drags at the same times in both modes.
